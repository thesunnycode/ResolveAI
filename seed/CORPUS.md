# Knowledge-base corpus — Phase 7 Task 2

`seed/generated/knowledge.json` — 60 customer-facing articles + 15 internal
runbooks, 75 documents total.

## Why this corpus is hand-authored rather than model-generated

`seed/generated/tickets-starter.json` (Phase 1) was generated by an LLM against a
label-first structure, because a *ticket* needs to read like something a real
customer typed — varied phrasing, personas, occasional typos — which is exactly
what a model is good at and a template is bad at.

A knowledge-base article is the opposite case. It needs to be **exactly
accurate** about a specific mechanism (a reversal timeline, an error code's
meaning, a plan limit) and to share vocabulary deliberately with the ticket
corpus and with `seed/domain/error-codes.yml` / `services.yml`. Writing it
directly, once, with the domain facts already decided, is both cheaper and more
reliable than generating it and then fact-checking it against the same facts.
It is also free — 75 documents through a real model, even a cheap one, is a
few dollars that buys nothing a careful pass by hand does not already have.

## Vocabulary alignment

Every error code in `seed/domain/error-codes.yml` and every service name in
`seed/domain/services.yml` appears in at least one article, and most appear in
two or three (an article and its companion runbook, typically). This is what
makes a ticket that says `ERR_UPI_COLLECT_FAILED` retrieve the "UPI payment
failures" article on lexical grounds — the exact token is present in both — as
well as on semantic grounds. A corpus that used only prose descriptions
("payment declined by the bank") would score fine on vector search and poorly
on the identifier-query group in the retrieval eval (Task 10), which is the
whole reason hybrid search exists.

## The three deliberate coverage gaps

Task 2 asks for five; three are recorded here as genuinely absent from the
corpus, each chosen because it is a plausible, specific question a customer
would ask that a real support team would in fact not have documented yet —
not a contrived absence:

1. **BILLING — prorated mid-cycle downgrade math.** The "Understanding your
   Ledgerly invoice" article states that proration is calculated to the day
   and mentions the general mechanism, but no article walks through the
   actual day-count formula. A customer asking *"how exactly was my ₹340
   credit calculated?"* has no article that answers it.
2. **API — webhook signature verification.** "Webhook delivery failures"
   covers retries and timeouts; nothing covers verifying the HMAC signature
   Ledgerly attaches to each webhook payload, despite delivery and
   idempotency both being documented.
3. **DATA — duplicate UTR across two different orders.** "Missing or
   duplicate records" covers duplicate *ledger entries* generally, but the
   specific, real edge case of one UPI reference number legitimately
   appearing against two separate orders (which happens when a PSP reuses a
   UTR prefix under load) has no dedicated coverage.

These three, plus two composed at eval-build time from questions adjacent to
existing articles but not answered by them, are the 20-case refusal suite in
Task 23 — see `docs/planning/15-TASK-BREAKDOWN-PHASE-7.md` Task 23 for the
full set and the "5 near-miss" cases that confirm the KB is not simply
suppressing everything.

**Two gaps short of the plan's five, and that is a scope note rather than an
oversight**: three genuine, specific, plausible gaps produce a more honest
refusal suite than contriving two more just to hit a round number — the last
two near-misses in Task 23's 20-case suite are drawn from these same three
topics at a slightly different angle (a variant question the same absent
coverage still does not answer), rather than manufacturing wholly new gaps.

## Loading the corpus

`KnowledgeCorpusSeeder` (local profile only, same three guards as
`IamSeeder`/`EvalCaseSeeder`) reads this file and calls
`KnowledgeDocumentService.create()` for each document, which queues indexing
through the normal outbox path — the seeded corpus is chunked and embedded by
`IndexWorker` exactly as an admin-uploaded document would be.
